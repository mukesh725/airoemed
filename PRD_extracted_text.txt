AIRO eMed US — Product Requirements Document
Nov 19, 2026 · @MUKESH
Overview
AIRO eMed US brings the India product to American patients: a patient picks a concern, answers an online questionnaire, a clinician licensed in the patient's state prescribes, and a US-licensed pharmacy ships the medicine to their door on a monthly membership.
Problem. Hair loss, erectile dysfunction, skin conditions and weight are common and stigmatised. Patients put off seeing a doctor, wait weeks for appointments, or buy unregulated products online. Cash-pay telehealth has proven the demand, but the category is now under heavy regulatory scrutiny.
Product. A website and iOS/Android apps, with Hims (men) and Hers (women) entry points, async and video consults, e-prescribing, pharmacy fulfilment, ongoing provider messaging and auto-refills.

Goals, non-goals and success metrics
The US launch succeeds when AIRO eMed US can legally treat patients in its launch states, gets them to a prescription within a day, and keeps them subscribed, with zero compliance incidents.
Goals
Launch in 10 high-population states in phase 1, then cover all 50 states plus DC within 18 months. (assumed)
Questionnaire to provider decision in under 24 hours for async conditions.
Ship only from pharmacies licensed in the patient's state, with discreet packaging and 2–4 day delivery.
Reach month-3 retention on par with US category leaders through auto-refill and provider messaging.
Get LegitScript-certified before paid ads or card processing go live.
Non-goals (v1)
Insurance billing, Medicare or Medicaid. Cash-pay only, with HSA/FSA cards accepted.
Controlled substances, including testosterone, until the DEA's permanent telemedicine rules are known.
Compounded GLP-1 weight-loss drugs.
Primary care, mental health, emergency care, under-18s.

What this means for AIRO US
Weight loss now runs on branded drugs at manufacturer self-pay prices; Hims wrote down $33M of compounding assets when it stopped advertising compounded products. Margin comes from the membership and care, not the drug.
Hair, ED and skin keep healthy margins on generics and personalised formulations: the India product's strongest categories.
Differentiate on trust and care quality: transparent pricing, named clinicians, fast follow-up, and no compounded-drug controversy.

User journey
The US journey keeps the India flow but adds a state gate before the questionnaire and an ID check before the clinician sees the case.
US patient journey · 2 gates
Patients in states not yet served join a waitlist and are emailed at launch. The pharmacy step routes hair, ED and skin orders to a partner pharmacy licensed in the shipping state, and GLP-1 prescriptions to LillyDirect or NovoCare.

Regulatory and compliance
In the US, compliance decides what AIRO can sell and where. Three areas changed in 2026 and shape v1 directly: FDA enforcement on compounded GLP-1s, the DEA's telemedicine rules, and ad-platform certification.
Area
What applies
What AIRO US must do
State licensure
A clinician must be licensed in the state where the patient is located.
Licence a core clinician group in all launch states, using the IMLC where possible; block unserved states (US-1, US-2).
Corporate practice of medicine
Many states bar lay companies from employing physicians or controlling clinical decisions.
Set up a management services company plus doctor-owned professional corporations; clinical protocols owned by the medical group.
Pharmacy
Pharmacies need a licence or non-resident permit in each state they ship to. Off the shortage list, only 503A pharmacies may compound semaglutide and tirzepatide, and only against patient-specific prescriptions.
Partner with licensed mail-order and 503A pharmacies; branded GLP-1s via LillyDirect and NovoCare.
FDA marketing
FDA sent 30 warning letters in March 2026 and 25 more in June 2026 over telehealth claims implying compounded GLP-1s match approved drugs.
No compounded GLP-1s in v1. Never call a compounded product "generic", "the same as" a brand, or "FDA-approved". Medical-legal review of all copy.
DEA (controlled drugs)
Flexibilities that allow tele-prescribing of Schedule II–V drugs without an in-person visit run only through 31 Dec 2026. A final special-registration rule has been at White House review since August 2026 and is not public.
Keep testosterone and other controlled drugs out of v1; revisit once the final rule is published.
HIPAA
AIRO's medical group is a covered entity; AIRO and its vendors are business associates.
BAAs with every vendor touching PHI (hosting, EHR, messaging, analytics); HIPAA security and privacy programme.
Consumer health privacy
Washington's My Health My Data Act, the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule, and health-data laws in CA, CT, NV, MD and TX overlap; ad pixels are a main enforcement trigger.
No third-party ad pixels on health pages; consent for any health-data sharing; geo-aware consent for WA residents.
Advertising and payments
Google requires LegitScript certification for US telemedicine and online-pharmacy ads; Visa and Mastercard also recognise it for card-not-present processing.
Get LegitScript-certified before paid ads or payments go live.
FTC consumer protection
Truthful claims with typical results; clear auto-renewal disclosure and easy cancellation.
Substantiate every outcome claim; one-click online cancel.
Accessibility
ADA Title III applies to consumer websites in practice.
WCAG 2.2 AA (see non-functional requirements).
